## One-liner

Kuul puts a student's whole campus life in one app: their timetable, their class group chats, their friends' free time, and what's happening at their university.

## The problem

Students in Mongolia run university life across scattered tools: timetables as screenshots, class chats in general-purpose messengers mixed with personal ones, and campus news and lost items in Facebook groups. Nothing knows who's actually a student at your school, or when your friends are free.

## What Kuul does

- **Timetable:** build your weekly schedule once, see it at a glance, and set it as your lock-screen wallpaper.
- **Chat:** course groups and direct messages with replies, reactions, pins and moderation, all built for classmates.
- **Campus:** a school-only feed for news, communities, lost and found, and safety alerts.
- **Friends:** see who's free right now, compare timetables to find a shared gap, and add friends by shaking phones together.
- **Trust by design:** sign-up uses your school email only, so everyone on Kuul is a verified student at your university. Kuul is 16+, with guardian consent under 18 and optional consents unticked by default.

## Design decisions

1. **School email as the only way in.** It's a small onboarding cost that makes the whole network trustworthy. You know everyone in your campus feed is real.
2. **The timetable as the social graph.** "Who's free now" and comparing timetables turn a boring utility into the reason you open the app with friends.
3. **Chat that feels native.** Hold menus, in-bubble reactions, swipe-to-reply and emoji-only messages are tuned to match the apps students already use daily. A campus app only wins if its chat feels as good as theirs.
4. **Privacy defaults for young users.** Age gating, guardian consent and unticked optional consents were designed into the flow from the start, not added later.
