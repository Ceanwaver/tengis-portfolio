## Education: teaching UI/UX design

A summer UI/UX programme I designed and taught for young students, built to make the fundamentals of design engaging and hands-on.

**The goal:** To teach the fundamentals of UI/UX in a way that lasts: students should leave thinking like designers, not just remembering a lesson. That meant treating the course itself as a design problem, with the students as its users.

**My approach:**
1. **Engagement first.** The most important thing was that the class never felt like ordinary schoolwork. Every lesson was designed to show that creative work, and making things you care about, is rewarding in itself.
2. **From idea to design.** Students arrive with plenty of ideas; the challenge is giving them form. Guided discussion and hands-on exercises turned their concepts into sketches on paper, then into working designs.
3. **Principles through practice.** Each session paired one core principle (hierarchy, layout, colour, user flows, usability) with a practical exercise. [Confirm or adjust the topics]
4. **Feedback as a habit.** Students presented their work, gave and received feedback, and iterated. [Confirm]

**The programme:** I designed it myself, the way I would design a product: around its users, tested in the room and refined as it ran.
- Curriculum: from understanding users to sketching, wireframing and visual design.
- Lessons: short explanations, real-world examples and plenty of time to make things.
- Activities: exercises and challenges that turned each principle into something students could see and use.
- Projects: each student took an idea of their own from a first sketch to a finished design.

**What it taught me:** Explaining design to newcomers strips it back to its essentials. Teaching made me a clearer communicator, a more patient collaborator and a stronger designer.
